# Module 18A: Non-TLR DAMP Sensing and Downstream Danger Signaling - Corpus Building Tracker

## Overview

This module assembles experiment-level evidence for damage-associated molecular patterns (DAMPs) and danger-sensing pathways that are not TLR-centered. It follows each DAMP from source and release context through sensor or receptor, adaptor or inflammasome assembly, downstream kinase/transcriptional or cell-death program, pathway intersection, perturbation, and phenotype.

The module is intentionally broader than a list of danger molecules. It distinguishes ligand release from receptor sensing, receptor signaling from inflammasome activation, and molecular inflammation from SCI tissue and functional outcomes. Module 17 owns TLR-centered cascade architecture; Module 18 captures non-TLR routes and records TLR intersections only when they are experimentally supported.

## Required Extraction Fields

Every full-text upgrade should capture: DAMP source cell and release mechanism; extracellular ligand identity and modification state; sensor/receptor; receptor compartment; adaptor or inflammasome assembly; kinase, transcription-factor, and cell-death outputs; perturbation specificity; cell type; species; injury or comparator model; timing and dose; pathway intersections; molecular/cellular/tissue/behavioral phenotypes; rescue or epistasis; direct versus inferred edge status; and figure/section provenance.

## Boundary Notes

- DAMP abundance, DAMP release, receptor engagement, and downstream pathway activation are separate evidence claims.
- NLRP3 activation is an output or convergence point, not a DAMP identity.
- Extracellular ATP, mtDNA, histones, S100 proteins, HMGB1, and metabolic signals can have multiple sensors and dose-dependent effects.
- A TLR intersection belongs in Module 18 only when the non-TLR DAMP route is independently tested; otherwise route it to Module 17 as TLR context.
