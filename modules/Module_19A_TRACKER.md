# Module 19A: Histone Modification and Chromatin-State Regulation in SCI - Corpus Building Tracker

## Overview

This module assembles experiment-level evidence for histone modifications and chromatin-state mechanisms relevant to spinal cord injury, neuroregeneration, inflammation, and repair. It follows the chain from injury or stimulus through histone mark, writer/eraser/reader, genomic locus or enhancer, transcriptional program, perturbation, and phenotype.

Module 14 remains the home for CRISPR-based epigenome-editing platforms. Module 19 captures the endogenous chromatin biology and mark-specific causal evidence that identifies which loci, cell states, and enzymes should be edited or pharmacologically modulated.

## Required Extraction Fields

Every full-text upgrade should capture: stimulus or injury; histone species and residue; modification and direction; writer/eraser/reader; genomic locus; assay and normalization; cell type; species; timing; perturbation type and specificity; transcriptional output; pathway intersection; molecular/cellular/tissue/behavioral phenotype; rescue or epistasis; direct versus inferred edge status; and figure/section provenance.

## Boundary Notes

- A histone mark is not automatically activating or repressive outside its locus and cell-state context.
- Global mark abundance and locus-specific occupancy are separate observations.
- Chromatin accessibility does not identify the responsible histone enzyme without perturbation or recruitment evidence.
- Histone lactylation should remain distinct from generic lactate exposure and from non-histone lysine lactylation.
