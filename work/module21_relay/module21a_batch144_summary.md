# Module 21A Batch 144 review summary

Batch 144 reviewed the 25 exact priority-8 queue rows covering Nodal receptor complexes, NPB/NPW non-cognate GPCR candidates, natriuretic-peptide receptor cross-reactivity and clearance, NPS non-cognate receptor candidates, and NPY/adrenergic interactions. Searches covered PubMed, PMC, publisher pages, Reactome, and bounded pair-specific queries, with primary research retained wherever an exact or explicitly bounded edge was available.

## Outcomes

- 3 reviewed receptor-proximal relay candidates: the two Nodal ACVR1C/type-II/CFC1 complexes and species-dependent CNP-NPR1 cross-activation.
- 3 reviewed binding-only records: ANP-NPR2 cross-reactivity and ANP/ CNP binding to the NPR3 clearance receptor.
- 1 reviewed functional-only record: NPY and alpha-2 adrenergic pathway cross-talk affecting NTS c-Fos output; no direct NPY-ADRA2A interaction was inferred.
- 18 reviewed unresolved records: NPB/NPW non-cognate receptor candidates, NPS non-cognate receptor candidates, and subtype-unresolved NPY/beta-adrenergic cases.
- Nodal rows retain SMAD2 and SMAD3 as terminal TFs because phosphorylation/nuclear signaling and dominant-negative perturbations were directly reported. No terminal TF was assigned to natriuretic-peptide, NPS, NPB/NPW, or NPY rows.

## Boundary decisions

Direct ligand binding, receptor-complex/pathway relay, and functional-output evidence were kept distinct. The exact ACVR1C-ACVR2A/ACVR2B-CFC1 Nodal complexes are supported by primary Nodal/ALK7 and Cripto studies plus the Reactome complex definition, but the full ternary composition is not represented as a single structural measurement. ANP-NPR2 and CNP-NPR1 were retained only with species/cross-reactivity limitations. NPR3 rows remain binding/clearance records rather than intracellular TF pathways. Broad GPCR coupling, coexpression, and pathway similarity were not promoted to direct NPB/NPW/NPS edges. NPY interactions with alpha-2 or beta-adrenergic signaling were not treated as direct molecular binding; beta-subtype evidence was insufficient for ADRB1 or ADRB2.
